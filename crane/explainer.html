Crane Sway

An overhead crane's load swings after every start and stop, and the operator waits before setting it down. Anti-sway systems cancel the swing with two half-sized pushes, half a swing apart, but only if they know the pendulum, including a sling length someone types in. When that's wrong, the swing comes back. We add TabPFN on top: it predicts where the load will be in 1 s from the crane's own recent motion, and before each move it chooses the anti-sway setting for this load. No training loop: new rows in the table are the adaptation. On a real lab crane its 1-s error dropped 3× after 5 s of an unseen load. In MuJoCo the crane finished 12 new loads 35% faster, and the live pick and place learned the right anti-sway setting within a few cycles.

0 · Problem, use case, solution

Problem statement

Overhead cranes lose time and safety to load swing, because anti-sway systems depend on a pendulum model that is often wrong. Every start and stop leaves the hanging load swinging. The operator must wait for it to settle before setting down, and a swinging load can hit racks or people. In the US, 154 of 297 crane-related deaths in 2011–17 were workers struck by an object or equipment (BLS). Anti-sway control cancels the swing only if it knows the pendulum length. That includes a sling length the operator types in, and it changes with every rigging, load shape and lift height. When it's wrong, the swing comes back and the time savings vanish.

Use case

  • Where: indoor overhead (bridge) cranes in steel service centres, warehouses, machine and fabrication shops, with many different loads per shift.
  • Who: crane operators (advice), maintenance and automation engineers (setup), plant managers (throughput).
  • Task: pick a load at one bay, carry it 10 m, place it in another bay between racks within ±5 cm.
  • Pain today: 20+ s of waiting per move when the anti-sway is mis-set, operators retyping sling lengths, near-misses with racks.

Solution

  • TabPFN reads a table of the crane's own recent motion (12 sensor and plan values every 0.1 s) and predicts where the load will be 1 s later, with a 90% range.
  • Before each move it chooses the anti-sway setting by predicting swing left and settle wait for every setting from 3 values (sway period, speed, tuning error).
  • No training loop: each finished move is one more row, so it adapts to new loads within a few cycles.
  • Fits existing cranes: it keeps the certified anti-sway mechanism and only replaces the typed-in guess. It can start in manual (advisory) mode.

0b · What exactly is predicted, and what TabPFN does

TabPFN answers one question, again and again: "where will the load be 1 s from now, and how sure am I?"

What is predicted

  • Model 1, the forecast: the horizontal offset of the load from the trolley, exactly 1.0 s later, in metres. It's asked every 0.1 s. The answer is a median (best guess) plus a 90% range.
  • Model 2, the anti-sway choice: for one candidate setting, the largest swing left after arriving (m) and the settle wait (s). It's asked once per setting before each move.
  • Why these are checkable: every prediction is about something the crane's own sensors will measure shortly after. 1 s later the true offset is known; after the move the real wait is known. So every prediction gets scored, and every row gets its true answer automatically, with nobody labelling data.

What TabPFN's role is, and isn't

  • It is the predictor. TabPFN is a model Prior Labs pretrained once, on millions of synthetic tables, to do one thing: given a table of examples with answers plus a new row, output the distribution of the new row's answer. We give it the crane's own table (3,000 fleet rows plus this crane's recent rows) and the current row.
  • "Fit" means "store the table". There are no training epochs, no gradient steps, and no retraining when a new load arrives. Adding the new load's rows is the adaptation (3× better after 5 s on the real lab crane).
  • It is not the controller. The trolley path, the hoist, the anti-sway mechanism and the safety limits stay classical. TabPFN never moves a motor and never sees the sling length. It supplies predictions; the crane's rules turn them into actions (draw the ghost, keep clear of the racks, pick the anti-sway setting).
  • Its edge over physics: a physics model is exact only with the right pendulum length, and the sling is unknown. TabPFN infers the real behaviour from the motion itself: 0.7 vs 4.7 cm error with a hidden long sling.

1 · Why loads swing

Swing is set by acceleration and timing, not by speed

When the bridge accelerates at a, the load lags behind by an angle of about a/g. It then swings around that angle. When the acceleration stops, it keeps swinging with an amplitude that depends on how long the push lasted compared with the swing period. Push for exactly one period and the swing cancels itself. Input shaping uses this effect. Move the sliders: the dips in the curve are the timings that leave no swing.

The curves are simulated live: a single push of length tₐ, then coasting, measuring the largest load offset afterwards. With a heavy load on a short sling (R large, L₂ small), mode 2 barely matters and the formula fits. Drag the sling to 2.6 m and the payload ratio to 1.6, as for light pallets on long slings. The shaper tuned for an entered 0.5 m sling now misses its period, and the second mode adds swing no single-mode shaper removes.

2 · How anti-sway works, and what we tune

Two half-sized pushes, half a swing apart, cancel the swing, if the timing is right

Computed with a linear pendulum: rope 4.5 m, real sling 2.0 m, acceleration 0.4 m/s² for 3 s. Grey = the swing without anti-sway (56 cm left after the push).

  1. Why the load swings. When the trolley accelerates, the load hangs back. When the acceleration stops, it keeps swinging at its natural period T = 2π√(L/g), about 5.3 s here, with almost nothing to stop it.
  2. Two half-sized pushes. Like pushing a child on a swing at the wrong moment to stop it. The ZV shaper (Singer & Seering 1990) splits each speed-up and slow-down into two pushes of half the size. Push 1 starts a half-size swing. Half a period later the load is at the far side, and push 2 starts an equal swing in the opposite phase. The two cancel (panel 2). The trolley still makes the same move; its speed just rises in two steps (panel 1), and the move takes about T/2 longer.
  3. Why it needs tuning. The cancellation only works if push 2 comes at exactly half the real period. The shaper computes the period from a length it assumes: rope (measured) + hook + sling (typed in by the operator) + half the load. With a 0.5 m sling assumed and 2.0 m real, push 2 comes 0.3 s early and 10 cm of swing survives (panel 3). Swing left = |cos(π/2 × real/assumed frequency)| × unshaped swing, 18% here. That's above the ±5 cm set-down limit, so the crane waits. A long sling also adds a second, faster hook-vs-load swing that no single shaper cancels.
  4. What we tune, and what we don't. We don't fine-tune TabPFN: its weights never change and nothing is trained. We tune one anti-sway number, the sling length it assumes, for each load. While the beam is lifted, one batched TabPFN call predicts, for every setting (off, 0.5 … 3.0 m), the swing left and the settle time, from a table of past moves. The crane takes the fastest setting whose 99% swing clears the racks. The real outcome then becomes one more row.

Live result (live_pick_place.py --auto-tune, real sling 2.0 m). Cycle 1, with only the 120 commissioning moves: TabPFN chose 0.5 m, giving a 69.5 s cycle with 23.7 s of waiting. Cycle 2, after that one move was in its table: it chose 1.5 m, giving 46.9 s, 1.0 s of waiting, set down 1 cm from centre. 1.5 m rather than 2.0 m isn't an error: a double pendulum's effective length isn't simply rope + sling, and 1.5 m cancelled best in its data.

3 · How TabPFN fits in

Three things decide where a hanging load goes next; TabPFN reads all three from one table row

A hanging load is a pendulum. Its next second depends on (1) how the load moves now, (2) how long the pendulum is, and (3) what push the trolley gives next. TabPFN gets these as one row of numbers every 0.1 s and answers where the load will be 1 s later, with a 90% range. It has no training loop: its "knowledge" is a table of past rows, and new rows are how it adapts.

Where the crane goes, and who decides

  • Pick and place points are given, not predicted. There are two set-down bays on the rail, at x = 1 m and x = 11 m. A job is "pick the beam at its bay, place it at the other bay". In a real hall these points come from the warehouse system or the operator.
  • The trolley's path is planned by the controller: speed up, cruise, brake, ZV-shaped by the anti-sway (section 2). Because the controller knows its own plan, the planned move is an input.
  • Heights come from geometry. The hook lowers until the grip touches the beam (rope = rail height − hook − sling − beam height) and lowers it onto the floor to place it.
  • TabPFN decides two things: it forecasts where the load will be in 1 s (all the time), and before each move it chooses the anti-sway setting (section 6).

4 · Real lab crane

On a real crane, the textbook pendulum is wrong in every run

I searched for public crane sway datasets and checked each one. One fits: a lab tower crane from Nazarbayev University, released on Mendeley Data under CC BY 4.0. It has 33 runs of 99 s each at 100 Hz. They cover trolley moves at 8 cable lengths (0.1–0.8 m), three payload shapes and hoisting. In every trolley run the measured swing is slower than 2π√(L/g) with the logged cable length. It behaves as if the rope were 12–17 cm longer, and up to 28 cm longer with a beam payload. The logged columns can't explain that offset; the crane's own swing data can.

Results: TabPFN on the real crane (run 2026-11-21)

When the load is new, TabPFN adapts within seconds, with no retraining. On the unseen 500 g beam, adding the first 5 s of the beam's own swing to the table cut TabPFN's 1-second error from 0.30 to 0.10 cm. Its 90% ranges went from holding 32% of outcomes to 91%. Standard machine learning (gradient-boosted trees), retrained on the same rows, reached 0.37 cm. A pendulum refitted on those rows stayed at 0.50 cm, because no single pendulum length describes a swinging beam.

How it was run. Rows at 10 Hz with 12 features a controller knows at time t: the swing now, 0.2 s and 0.5 s ago, swing rate, the largest swing in the last 2 s, trolley position, speed and acceleration, cable length and its rate, and the planned trolley displacement over +0.5 s and +1 s. Targets: the swing angle 0.5 s and 1.0 s ahead. Whole runs are held out, never random rows. TabPFN-3.5 (n_estimators=1, fit_with_cache) gives quantiles 0.05 / 0.5 / 0.95. Both pendulums are integrated along the logged trolley motion; the fitted one grid-searches length offset and damping on the context runs. Error in cm = |angle error| × cable length. Scripts: crane/real_data.py, exp1_real.py, exp2_real.py; results in crane/results/. Caveats: one run per condition, one seed. The beam run has a single trolley move at the start, so test B is mainly the free swing of an unknown load. This is a lab-scale tower crane.

5 · MuJoCo crane

The same idea on a full-size crane in a physics engine

We built an industrial overhead crane in MuJoCo: a trolley on a rail, a rope to a hook, slings to a beam, and racks beside two set-down bays. Then we gave it a load it had never seen: a light 400 kg, 2.4 m beam on 2.6 m slings, while the anti-sway is still set for 0.5 m. Every 0.1 s, TabPFN predicts where the load will be 1 s later. Every 5 s, the rows whose outcome is already known are added to its table. Nothing is retrained.

How it was made. The physics was checked first: the free-swing period is 5.11 s in MuJoCo, against 5.06 s from the double-pendulum formula in section 1. The small difference comes from the beam's own inertia. The trolley follows its planned trapezoid moves under feedforward plus PD control, within 1.1 cm. The context is 3,000 rows sampled from 30 simulated "fleet" jobs on short slings (0.5–1.5 m) with 1–3 t beams, plus the held-out job's own rows. The features are the same kind as for the real data: load offset now, 0.2 s and 0.5 s ago, its rate, the largest swing in the last 2 s, trolley position, speed and acceleration, rope length, payload from the load cell, and the planned trolley move. The sling length is hidden. TabPFN-3.5 uses n_estimators=1 and gives quantiles 0.05 / 0.5 / 0.95. Caveats: it's a simulation, with one held-out job and one seed. The video was rendered offline: each refit takes 7–8 s on our GTX 1650 laptop GPU, so this laptop could not run it live (the team benchmark had about 0.3 s fits on an RTX 4050). Scripts: crane/mujoco_crane.py, mujoco_demo.py; video results/mujoco_demo.mp4.

Live pick and place (crane/live_pick_place.py, opens a MuJoCo window): the beam is a separate body. The hook lowers on the hoist, grabs the beam, lifts, travels, waits until the swing is within ±5 cm for 1 s, lowers it into the other bay and releases it, then picks it up again. TabPFN's 1-s ghost runs during travel and settling. With a real 2.0 m sling and the anti-sway set for the entered 0.5 m, a cycle took 69.5 s (23.7 s of settle wait). With the anti-sway set for 2.0 m (← / → keys), it took 47 s (1.0–1.6 s of settle wait), placed within ±5 cm. Simulation, one run each.

6 · Closed loop

The crane uses the table to choose every move: 35% faster

This is how far a table takes the crane. Before each move, while the load is hooked, the crane makes one batched TabPFN call over 28 candidates (7 speeds × 4 anti-sway tunings). For each, TabPFN predicts the residual swing (median and 99th percentile) and the settle time. The crane takes the fastest expected cycle whose 99th-percentile swing stays inside the 0.45 m clearance. After the move, its real outcome is one more row. We ran 12 new loads on long slings (1.8–3.0 m), never in its 150 commissioning moves, against industry anti-sway set for the entered 0.5 m sling.

7 · Could we just time the set-down?

Timing the set-down alone isn't enough; the swing has to be killed

A tempting shortcut is to leave the crane's control alone and use TabPFN only to say when to start the final lowering. Lower as soon as the load is predicted to be inside the ±5 cm target 1 s later (the lowering time), instead of waiting until it has stayed there for a full second. We tested this on the 12 industry anti-sway moves from 6, using every saved 0.1 s of their settle phase.

When to start lowering (industry anti-sway moves)Fired earlyLoad inside ±5 cm at touchdownSideways speed at touchdown (median)Safe touchdown (also < 5 cm/s)
Physics predicts inside ±5 cm12/124/1213 cm/s0/12
TabPFN: whole 90% range inside ±5 cm12/1212/1215 cm/s0/12
TabPFN: inside for 5 predictions in a row (0.5 s)12/1212/1214 cm/s0/12
  • TabPFN knows where the load will be: its early touchdowns land inside ±5 cm on 12 of 12 moves; the physics model with the wrong sling manages 4 of 12.
  • But none is safe. A 15 cm swing passes through the ±5 cm zone for about 0.5 s each crossing, moving sideways at about 15 cm/s. Landing then knocks the load sideways. Today's rule (inside for a full second) implicitly requires the swing itself to be small, and no amount of timing changes that.
  • A safe timing advisor can save at most about the last second of confirmation plus the lowering overlap. The big gain comes from killing the swing, which is what the closed loop in 6 does by re-tuning the anti-sway (35% faster).
  • Where timing does win: release jobs. Magnet and grab cranes drop scrap or bulk material into a hopper mid-swing, so only the landing point matters: position at release + sideways speed × fall time. TabPFN's accurate position prediction is exactly what that needs. It's our next demo, not built yet.
  • Script: crane/setdown_advisor.py; results crane/results/setdown_advisor.json. Simulation, 12 moves, one seed.

8 · How it is handled today

Operator skillThe operator times "catch" moves by eye and waits for the swing to die downdepends on the person; slow with long ropes; fatigue
Open-loop input shapingShapes acceleration and deceleration so the swing cancels; for example Konecranes Sway Control limits swing by controlling bridge and trolley acceleration, and the operator can set a sling-length parameter (Konecranes)needs the right pendulum model; a wrong sling entry or double-pendulum rigging brings the swing back

11 · Limits and next steps

What is shown, what is not

  • Real data is lab-scale: a tower crane with cables under 1 m and payloads under 1 kg, one run per condition. The industrial-size results are MuJoCo simulation, one seed each.
  • Collisions: none in any strategy, so the gains shown are time, not safety.
  • Timing the set-down alone does not work (section 7). The swing has to be killed.
  • The live auto-tune makes early mistakes before its own rows accumulate.
  • Next: a magnet or grab release demo (where landing-point prediction is the whole job), then real industrial crane logs in shadow mode, and faster GPU refits for live control.
  • TabPFN-3.5 weights are non-commercial; a product needs a Prior Labs licence.
